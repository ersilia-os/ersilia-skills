## The models

### `eos5jv3` · MycoPermeNet mycobacterial membrane permeation

**Irene Lepori, M. Sloan Siegrist and 18 colleagues** — University of Massachusetts Amherst · University of Virginia · Amherst College · Nature Microbiology 2026

Predicts how readily a small molecule crosses the mycomembrane, the outer membrane of *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* that is a principal barrier to antibiotic entry, returning a standardised permeation residual in which lower values mean higher permeability. The model served is MycoPermeNet-v2, which fuses a graph neural network embedding with normalised RDKit descriptors and adds Noisy Student self-distillation to cope with scarce labels, learning from the 1,572 azide-tagged compounds profiled by the PAC-MAN click-chemistry assay in *M. tuberculosis* and *M. smegmatis*. Credit splits across two papers: the assay, the dataset and the original MycoPermeNet are Lepori and colleagues in *Nature Microbiology*, which is the paper the table above cites, while the v2 architecture actually served is Evbarunegbe, Wa, Karn and Green in *J. Chem. Inf. Model.* (10.1021/acs.jcim.5c02435), who report a modest but statistically significant RMSE improvement over the original. The model card sets a narrow applicability domain — small azide-tagged compounds of roughly 82–570 Da — outside which predictions may leave the −3.1 to +1.6 output range.

Paper: [10.1038/s41564-026-02412-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-026-02412-5) · Authors' code: https://github.com/Nevbarunegbe/Mycomembrane-permeability-project · Run it: `ersilia fetch mycopermenet` · Licence: MIT
